Doctrine III · the tree

The Tree of the Stack

The old diagram arranges ten emanations on three pillars: one of force, one of form, and one of balance between them. An agent stack has the same shape and the same argument, because everything that goes wrong in it is a failure to reconcile what we want with what the machine will actually do.

Ten stations, from the intent nobody can see to the metal that cannot be retried. Twenty-two paths, each a real dependency rather than a decoration. Choose a station to read it.

On the three pillars

The right-hand pillar is what we want: the prompt, the tools, the tokens we are willing to spend. It is expansive by nature and has no instinct for its own limits.

The left-hand pillar is what is permitted: the context we actually have, the rate limits, the log that will or will not be there afterwards. It is restrictive by nature and is usually discovered rather than designed.

The middle pillar is what happens: intent at the top, the model at the centre, the process and the metal at the bottom. Every genuine incident in an agent system is a place where the middle pillar turned out to be governed by the left while everyone was looking at the right.
