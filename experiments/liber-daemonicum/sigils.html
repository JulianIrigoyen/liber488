The Sigil Workshop

A statement of intent goes in; a figure comes out. The traditional method is to strike the repeated letters from a sentence and fuse what survives into a single glyph. Two of the three methods here do something close to that. The third does what a machine would do instead, which is to hash the sentence and draw the hash.

All three are deterministic. The same sentence yields the same figure on any machine, in any browser, forever. That reproducibility is the only claim this page makes, and it is the same claim the tradition makes about a true seal: that it belongs to its name and to nothing else.

The statement

Write it in the present tense

The convention is to phrase the intent as accomplished rather than wished for. Whether that matters is outside this cabinet's competence; that it produces a better sentence is not in dispute.

Downloads as SVG at 640px, on a transparent ground, with no raster step anywhere in the pipeline. It is a drawing, not a picture of one.

What this is and is not: a deterministic function from a string to a figure. It will give you the same seal for the same sentence every time, which is genuinely useful for marking things - a service, a run, a build - with a mark that can be regenerated rather than stored. Any further claim belongs to the tradition, not to this page.
