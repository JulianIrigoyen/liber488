Ask once, and throw it yourself

The oracle is not a lookup table with a shuffle in front of it. You hold a question, you throw the coins with your own hand, and the figure that comes up is built from that particular act at that particular moment. This tool keeps that structure: the question is committed before anything is thrown, each of the six lines takes its own throw, and the coins are decided by your gesture — the timing and path of your hand — rather than by a random number generator running while you watch.

Ask about a situation you are in, not for a yes or no. What should I understand about… works better than Will…

These are not the same oracle. Yarrow makes moving yang three times likelier than moving yin; coins make them equal. Choose before you begin — it cannot be changed mid-consultation.

Press and hold, move your hand while you hold, and let go to throw. The movement is the throw — nothing is decided until you release.

On asking

A question you have already decided the answer to will produce an answer you have already decided. The tradition's warning is blunt: ask once and receive an answer; ask the same thing three times and you are not asking, you are avoiding.

The reading is in the changing lines. A cast with none is a description of a situation that is not moving. A cast with several is a situation coming apart and reassembling, and the second hexagram is where it is headed.

What is original here

All 64 hexagram texts and all 384 changing-line texts on this site were written for it. They are interpretations, not translations, and they do not reproduce any translator's wording. The framework they fill — King Wen numbering, trigram composition, the six line positions — is the classical structure, roughly three thousand years old.

For the source, read a real translation. Wilhelm/Baynes is the one that shaped the Western reception; Legge is older and public domain; Rutt and Shaughnessy are the ones to read if you want the Bronze Age text rather than its Confucian commentary.

Where the numbers come from

Not from a random number generator. Each throw is derived from the motion samples your own hand produces during the shake — sub-millisecond timings, pointer path, and accelerometer data if you enable it. Those samples pass through SHA-256, which acts as a randomness extractor: it preserves the entropy you supplied while producing uniform bits, so the traditional 1/8, 3/8, 3/8, 1/8 distribution comes out exactly right.

What that means: the deciding act is yours, it runs through your body, and it is not repeatable. What it does not mean: that this makes the oracle work. That is not a question a web page gets to settle, and this one does not pretend to.

Privacy

Your questions and readings are kept in this browser's local storage and nowhere else. Nothing is transmitted, and nothing is stored on any server. Clearing the journal removes them permanently.
